## 2. Program purpose and scope

The Human-Scale AI Incubator seeks working AI technologies that provide alternatives to dominant, highly centralized, resource-intensive AI systems.

The program is focused on AI systems designed around the actual needs, people, communities, and operating conditions of a defined problem.

The program's three required principles are:

### 2.1 Small

Projects should:

- Use small or appropriately sized models and make efficient use of compute, energy, and infrastructure.
- Use local or low-power devices where appropriate.
- Match model and infrastructure requirements to the actual task and deployment environment.
- Demonstrate how technical efficiency makes the product more capable, accessible, affordable, private, or practical for its intended use.
- Explain which model or models are being used and why their size is appropriate.

Mozilla notes that models as small as 7B parameters can work well for text applications; local voice applications may require models up to 32B. These are examples, not universal mandatory model-size limits.

### 2.2 Intentional

Projects should:

- Give users visibility into how their data is collected, stored, transmitted, and used.
- Give users, communities, and creators meaningful control over personal data and intellectual property.
- Enable meaningful user or community agency.
- Where appropriate, allow systems or datasets to be configured, adapted, forked, owned, or governed by the community.

### 2.3 Tailored

Projects should:

- Address a specific problem, community, language, or real-world context.
- Identify clearly who the technology is being built for.
- Show active dialogue with the people who will use the technology.
- Demonstrate how the needs and context of those people shaped the product, technical design, functionality, or deployment.
- Give particular consideration to resource-constrained regions, people with disabilities, and underserved or vulnerable populations.
- Where possible, include people with relevant lived experience in maintaining or building the project.

**All three principles are expected.** The way each principle is expressed may differ according to the technology and problem.

## 3. What Mozilla is looking for

The call seeks **seven projects** that:

- Have working technology already deployed with the people it is designed to serve, including at a small scale or through an early pilot.
- Have early evidence of product-community fit.
- Have meaningful technical, product, community, deployment, or sustainability work remaining.
- Use AI or machine learning as a core part of the solution.
- Offer a technically credible alternative to dominant AI approaches in resource use, privacy, user agency, or concentration of power.
- Have the potential to create meaningful public benefit.
- Can make progress during the Incubator period and contribute useful outputs to the wider field.

### 3.1 Evidence of product-community fit

Applicants do **not** need to have fully achieved product-community fit. They do need early evidence that people value the technology and want to participate in its future.

Mozilla identifies examples such as:

- **Users:** People are using the technology and returning to it.
- **Contributors:** People contribute code, content, expertise, or other meaningful work.
- **Community:** The people served participate in shaping product priorities, design, or governance.
- **Partners:** Organizations want to pilot, deploy, collaborate on, or fund the work.
- **Downstream adoption:** Other builders use the technology in their own work, or people contribute time, money, effort, or other value to support it.

Mozilla looks for early signals and meaningful potential to deepen product-community fit during the Incubator.

## 4. What is not eligible or not sought

The call states that Mozilla is generally **not** looking for:

1. Concepts without working technology, including ideas that exist only as wireframes or prototypes.
2. Standalone governance or licensing models, policy frameworks, or research projects without a working technology component.
3. Technologies without users, contributors, or another meaningful signal of early product-community fit.
4. Generic chatbot applications that simply apply existing AI capabilities to a new topic or audience.
5. Projects using frontier-class models where a much smaller model would be sufficient.
6. Projects that merely call a large commercial model without a compelling task-specific justification.

Additional FAQ clarifications:

- The project must use AI as a **core part** of its solution. Adding an AI feature to an otherwise unrelated project does not make it eligible.
- Simple prototypes using off-the-shelf technology, such as a chatbot that uses an LLM to provide information, are not what the program seeks.
- The program is primarily technical. Projects grounded mainly in philosophical reflection, academic exchange, cultural production, or community education rather than technical development are not eligible for this Incubator.
- If a large model is used, the application should clearly justify why it is necessary for the problem.

## 5. Applicant eligibility

### 5.1 Applicant types

The Foundation's general eligibility guidelines identify these applicant types:

- Nonprofit organizations.
- Individuals carrying out a charitable project aligned with Mozilla's mission.
- For-profit companies and other organizations carrying out a charitable project aligned with Mozilla's mission.
- Accredited academic institutions.

The Foundation generally considers applicants and projects from around the world, subject to the program-specific conditions and legal restrictions.

### 5.2 Charitable purpose is required

Every funded project must serve a clear charitable purpose and deliver public benefit beyond the applicant or its owners.

The Foundation identifies examples of charitable purposes including:

- Educating the public.
- Advancing scientific research.
- Protecting legally secured human rights.
- Protecting the environment.
- Combating prejudice and discrimination.
- Promoting the arts.
- Providing direct relief to underserved communities.

The benefit must flow outward to the public, not primarily back to the applicant's bottom line, shareholders, or owners.

Using an open-source licence or referring to a social good is not, by itself, enough to establish charitable purpose if the underlying purpose is commercial.

### 5.3 For-profit applicants

For-profit entities are not automatically excluded. They must demonstrate that the specific funded project is genuinely charitable.

Applicants applying as for-profit entities should clearly explain the charitable case, including tangible benefits for people outside the team.

The Foundation cannot fund work whose primary purpose is:

- Commercial gain.
- Profit maximization.
- Generating shareholder value.

Grants to for-profit entities carry additional spending limitations. The eligibility guidelines state, for example, that grant funds may not be used to purchase valuable capital equipment above applicable limits, such as computers, servers, or GPUs, or to provide unrestricted general operating support.

### 5.4 Non-US nonprofit applicants

A nonprofit outside the United States may qualify through an equivalency determination certificate.

The guidelines clarify that local nonprofit registration and a US equivalency determination are different things. An equivalency determination is not required before applying; Mozilla may be able to help obtain one through NGOsource if the applicant is selected.

### 5.5 Individuals, informal groups, and fiscal sponsorship

- Individuals may apply for charitable projects aligned with Mozilla's mission.
- Informal groups may be considered under the Foundation's eligibility framework.
- Teams that are not themselves recognized as tax-exempt may use a fiscal sponsor.
- The FAQ specifically says fiscal sponsorship is an option and is often recommended for teams not yet recognized as tax-exempt.

### 5.6 Legal and payment requirements

Every applicant must be able to:

- Legally receive grant funds from a US-based organization by check, ACH, or wire transfer.
- Legally enter into a grant agreement with Mozilla Foundation.
- Receive payments in USD through a bank account that can accept USD.

If funds are received in another currency, the applicant is responsible for exchange-rate differences and bank fees.

The FAQ adds that any selected team must be able to receive funding from a US-based 501(c)(3) organization.

### 5.7 Conflicts and prohibited applicants or activities

The Foundation cannot fund:

- Applicants located in a country on the OFAC sanctions list.
- Applicants more than 50% owned by an entity in a sanctioned country.
- Applicants named on a sanctions list.
- Political campaign activity, including supporting or opposing candidates for public office.
- Lobbying or efforts to influence specific legislation.
- Terrorism, violence, or illegal activity.
- Work whose primary purpose is commercial gain, profit maximization, or shareholder value.
- Applicants with a conflict of interest involving Mozilla staff, including specified immediate-family, employee, contractor, leadership, or shareholder relationships.

The Foundation's full eligibility guidelines govern the detailed restrictions.

## 6. Evaluation criteria

Projects that meet eligibility requirements are evaluated across **six criteria**.

### 6.1 Human-Scale AI

- Does the project offer a compelling expression of Human-Scale AI?
- Does it reflect the Small, Intentional, and Tailored principles in ways appropriate to the problem?

### 6.2 Technical viability

- Is the technology novel?
- Does it demonstrate real momentum?
- Does the team have the capacity to execute?
- Is the technical approach appropriate for the problem, budget, and grant term?

### 6.3 Problem-community clarity

- Does the team understand the problem?
- Is it clear who the project is solving the problem for?
- Is there evidence of dialogue with potential users?
- Is there evidence of a real-world need?

### 6.4 Product-community fit

- Is there evidence that people want, need, use, contribute to, or otherwise value the technology?
- Is there a clear opportunity to deepen product-community fit?

### 6.5 Impact and values

- Does the project have clear potential for public benefit and meaningful impact for the communities it serves?
- Does it enable greater agency, transparency, openness, or community control?

### 6.6 Sustainability potential

- Is there a plausible path for the work to endure in a way that aligns with its values?
- Is the team open to exploring different sustainability models?

### 6.7 Cohort-level considerations

Mozilla's evaluation also considers:

- How the technology has been adapted for a specific language, community, or setting.
- The novelty of the technology.
- The overall mix of geographies, communities, applications, and technical approaches across the finalists.

The call does not publish numerical weights or a point-based scoring rubric for the six criteria.

## 7. Selection and review process

- Applications are reviewed through a **multi-stage process**.
- The primary review occurs at the **Initial Project Proposal** stage.
- Only a small number of shortlisted applicants are invited to submit a **full application**.
- The call does not publish a detailed scoring formula, stage-by-stage weighting, or a guaranteed number of finalists.
- All applicants are expected to receive an update on their submission status by **late February 2027**.
- Due to the high volume of submissions, Mozilla says it cannot provide individual application updates.

## 8. Funding and Incubator terms

### 8.1 Initial funding

Selected projects receive:

- **US$300,000**
- **12 months** of intensive support
- Support beginning in **April 2027**

### 8.2 Possible follow-on funding

Projects demonstrating strong progress may be eligible for:

- Up to **US$200,000** in additional funding.
- Two additional years of bespoke support.

The maximum potential support is therefore **US$500,000 over three years**.

Follow-on funding is not automatic. It considers:

- Progress on the core technology.
- Evidence of product-community fit.
- Meaningful public benefit.
- A clear roadmap for what another two years of support could unlock.

### 8.3 Support provided

The Incubator describes the following support:

- Cohort of international builders.
- Project-specific coaching, workshops, connections, and outside expertise.
- Regular one-to-one support from a Mozilla Program Officer.
- Access to Mozilla's global network of technologists, activists, communities, partners, and funders.
- Opportunities to present work at Mozilla Festival and the Incubator Showcase.

### 8.4 Participation expectations

Selected teams are expected to:

- Participate actively in cohort calls, workshops, coaching, and other offerings.
- Attend required sessions, generally limited to one or two one-hour virtual meetings per month.
- Share challenges, approaches, resources, and lessons with cohort peers.
- Work in the open and share outputs and learnings with the cohort, served communities, and wider field.
- Leave behind something of value others can use or build on.

Possible public-facing outputs include open-source components, documentation, datasets, research, shared methodologies, community access, educational resources, or public workshops, as appropriate to the project.

## 9. Application rules and administrative conditions

- **Language:** Applications must be submitted in English.
- **Translation:** Translation tools are permitted.
- **AI assistance:** AI tools may be used to brainstorm, clarify language, or assist with translation.
- **Originality:** Submitted content must ultimately reflect the applicant's own original work, ideas, and voice.
- **AI-generated responses:** Use of AI or translation tools should remain supportive. Applications that appear heavily or entirely AI-generated, or that do not show authentic engagement with the application questions, may receive further review.
- **Submission finality:** Once an application is submitted, it can no longer be edited or updated. Submit only when final.
- **Saved applications:** A saved application can be resumed using the unique link sent by email and the email/password used during the save process.
- **Lost resume link:** Check spam. If the link cannot be found, the FAQ directs applicants to submit a new application.
- **Multiple applications:** More than one application is allowed for multiple projects, but Mozilla strongly encourages submitting only the project that best fits the call. Larger institutions such as universities may submit multiple applications if each has a different lead or PI.
- **Indirect costs:** For grants made to organizations, indirect costs are typically allowed up to 10%, subject to the Foundation's policy.

## 12. Anchor projects published by Mozilla

Mozilla lists three preselected anchor teams as examples of Human-Scale AI practice. These are not additional eligibility categories.

1. **Tiles** — An open, private AI commons for education, including offline learning tools, open learning materials, and a governance framework. The published description highlights quantized open-weight models, local execution, encrypted peer-to-peer inference, and offline deployment.
2. **Family Intelligence** — An open protocol for private shared household AI memory, with encrypted storage and local search and reasoning on users' devices. The published description includes an Apache 2.0 reference implementation, threat model, and independent security review.
3. **DAIR (Distributed AI Research Institute)** — Renewably powered shared compute and open infrastructure intended to help smaller, values-aligned organizations run models without relying on hyperscale cloud providers. The published description includes plans for open-source infrastructure and operator guides.

## 13. Final compliance checklist

Use this as a rules-based check against the published call:

- [ ] The project is working technology, not only an idea, wireframe, or concept.
- [ ] The technology is already deployed with intended users or has an early real-world pilot.
- [ ] There is early evidence of product-community fit.
- [ ] AI or machine learning is core to the solution.
- [ ] The project demonstrates all three principles: Small, Intentional, and Tailored.
- [ ] Model size and compute use are appropriate to the task and deployment context.
- [ ] The application explains the model choices and justifies any large-model use.
- [ ] Users can understand how their data is collected, stored, transmitted, and used.
- [ ] Users or communities have meaningful agency and control.
- [ ] The target community and real-world problem are clearly identified.
- [ ] The technology reflects input from the people it is designed to serve.
- [ ] The project has a credible technical execution plan and capable team.
- [ ] The project has a clear public-benefit and charitable purpose.
- [ ] The applicant can legally receive US-based grant funds and enter a grant agreement.
- [ ] The project and applicant meet sanctions, conflict-of-interest, and prohibited-activity rules.
- [ ] The proposal is in English and reflects the applicant's original work and voice.
- [ ] The final submission is completed before the deadline.
- [ ] The team can meet the Incubator participation and open-sharing expectations if selected.
